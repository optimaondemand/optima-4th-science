🔑 Teacher Answer Key

Suggested answers, scoring notes, and how to apply the common rubric.

Scoring approach. Each question shows ONE possible strong answer. Student answers may use different wording but should hit the same key ideas. Use the score guide under each question to decide level: 0 = blank/incorrect, 1 = partial, 2 = full.

Part A · Map & Materials Investigation

Q1

Renewable vs. nonrenewable on the map.

Sample answer
The renewable resource is in the SOUTH region — fresh water refills through rain and the water cycle. The nonrenewable resource could be the NORTH (phosphate) or CENTRAL (limestone) — both take millions of years to form, so they can't be quickly replaced.
ScoringProficient: names a correct renewable + a correct nonrenewable region with reasoning. 1 pt each.
Q2

What the ★ symbol means.

Sample answer
The ★ symbol marks phosphate (a mineral resource) found in the North Region of the map. The map tells me that the North Region has a different landform (rolling hills) than the rest — and that's where phosphate is mined in Florida.
Scoring1pt for symbol meaning + 1pt for "what it tells you."
Q3

Hardest and softest sample.

Key
Sample C is hardest (Mohs 10). Sample A is softest (Mohs 3). The HARDNESS property (Mohs scale) tells us — higher number means harder.
Scoring1pt each for correct hardest/softest. 1pt for naming hardness/Mohs as the property used.
Q4

Is Sample B real gold?

Sample answer
Sample B is NOT real gold. Real gold leaves a yellow streak. This sample leaves a greenish-black streak — that's the giveaway for fool's gold (pyrite). The streak test is one of the most reliable ways to tell minerals apart.
ScoringProficient: states it's not gold + uses the streak data. Advanced: names pyrite.
Q5

Benefit + cost of one resource.

Sample answer (limestone)
I picked limestone. The benefit is that we use it to build roads, sidewalks, and buildings all over Florida. The environmental cost is that quarries leave big holes in the ground, kick up a lot of dust, and destroy habitat where animals used to live.
ScoringProficient: names a clear benefit AND a clear cost from the table.
Q6

Why solar is renewable but phosphate isn't.

Sample answer
Solar energy is renewable because the Sun keeps shining — it doesn't run out from us using it. Phosphate is nonrenewable because it formed in the ground over millions of years from ancient sea-creature remains. Once we dig it all out, it won't be replaced for many human lifetimes.
ScoringProficient: includes the TIMESCALE idea for each.
Q7

Limestone protection strategy.

Sample answer
One protection strategy is to refill old quarries with soil and replant native Florida plants when mining is finished. This helps because the land can become wildlife habitat again, and the dust and erosion drop way down.
ScoringAccept any reasonable strategy (refill, native plant restoration, water capture, dust control, smaller mining areas, etc.) tied to a real environmental cost.
Q8

Where Florida sand came from.

Sample answer
Florida's sand came from rocks that broke down over a long time. WEATHERING broke big rocks into smaller and smaller pieces. EROSION moved those tiny pieces along rivers and oceans until they were deposited on the beaches we see today.
ScoringProficient: uses both weathering AND erosion correctly.
CER · Claim
Sample
Using a natural resource doesn't ALWAYS hurt the environment, but it usually has some cost.
CER · Evidence
Sample
Evidence 1: Solar energy is renewable but solar panels still take materials to make. Evidence 2: Limestone is heavily used but quarries cause dust, holes, and habitat loss.
CER · Reasoning
Sample
The evidence shows that EVEN renewable resources have some impact when we collect or build with them. Communities have to choose resources with the smallest impact and use protection strategies to reduce harm.
Q9 · Reflection
Open-ended
Accept any thoughtful answer naming a specific resource (water, electricity, fuel, paper, food, etc.), its cost, and a realistic family action (turn off lights, reuse water, recycle, etc.).

Common misconceptions to watch for:
  • Confusing the words renewable and reusable — clarify that renewable means refilled by nature.
  • Thinking ALL metals are the same — use the streak test discussion to differentiate pyrite from gold.
  • Treating sand/limestone as separate from rocks — both come from rock-cycle processes.
