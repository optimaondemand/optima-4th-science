📘 The Lesson: Magnets Have Two Poles
Opposites attract; same pushes away
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Poles: North and South

Every magnetMagnetPulls some metals; has two poles. has two ends called polesPoleThe two ends of a magnet.: a NORTH pole and a SOUTH pole, always at opposite ends. So what happens if you snap a magnet in half? You’ll find out below.

🧲 Two Magnets, Two Outcomes
The rule never changes: N + S = pull together. N + N or S + S = push apart.

When you bring two magnet poles together, here's the rule: opposite poles attractAttractPull together. (N + S = pull together). Same poles repelRepelPush apart. (N + N = push apart; S + S = push apart).

Scientists use this rule to find a pole they can’t see. Try it on a magnet whose letters wore off.

Part 2 · Magnetic vs. Non-Magnetic Materials

A magnet doesn't pull on EVERYTHING. It only attracts materials that are magneticMagneticMaterial a magnet pulls.: mainly iron, nickel and most kinds of steel.

So is every metal magnetic? Sweep the tray and see.

That’s why a magnet sticks to your fridge door (steel) but not to a soda can (aluminum).

Part 3 · The Invisible Pull Zone

The invisible pull zone around a magnet is its magnetic fieldMagnetic fieldInvisible area of pull or push.. Earth itself is a giant magnet! A compass needle is a tiny magnet, so Earth’s magnetic field turns it to point toward Earth’s magnetic north, near the North Pole.

Part 4 · The Magnet Treasure Hunt

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Two magnets are held with NORTH pole facing NORTH pole. What happens?

AThey snap together
BNothing happens
CThey push apart
🚫 Right! Same poles REPEL — they push each other away.
Same poles always REPEL — they push apart. Only OPPOSITE poles (N + S) attract and snap together.
Q2

Which material does a magnet ATTRACT?

APlastic
BA steel paperclip
CAn aluminum soda can
🔩 Right! Steel contains iron — and iron is one of the few materials a magnet attracts.
Magnets only attract iron, nickel, and steel. Plastic and aluminum aren't magnetic — a magnet ignores them.
Q3

Why does a compass needle point north?

AEarth is a giant magnet, and the needle aligns with its magnetic field
BThe needle is heavier on one end
CThe Sun pulls on it
🧭 Right! Earth's own magnetic field makes the tiny magnet inside a compass needle line up north-south.
The compass needle is itself a small magnet. Earth's magnetic field is what pulls it to point toward the planet's magnetic north.
