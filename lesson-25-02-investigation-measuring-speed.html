📘 The Lesson: Speed Needs Distance AND Time
Measure both, and you can compare anything
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Speed = How Far ÷ How Long

SpeedSpeedHow fast something moves. is how fast something moves. To measure it, you need two things: how far it went (distanceDistanceHow far.) and how long it took (timeTimeHow long.).

"Maya ran 30 feet in 5 seconds" tells you her speed in a useful way. "Maya ran 30 feet" alone doesn't — she could have been racing or strolling. "Maya ran for 5 seconds" alone doesn't — she could have walked or sprinted. You need BOTH numbers.

🛑
Stop & Think
"Maya runs 30 feet in 5 seconds. Jordan runs 30 feet in 10 seconds. Who is faster? How did you decide?"

Part 2 · Comparing Speeds Fairly

To compareCompareLook at side by side. two speeds fairly, you run a fair testFair testSame setup for everything. — everything stays the same except the thing you're testing. Same start line. Same finish line. Same surface.

  • Same distance, different times → shorter time wins (faster).
  • Same time, different distances → longer distance wins (faster).
  • Different both → compare distance ÷ time. A car going 60 miles in 1 hour is faster than one going 90 miles in 2 hours.
  • Collect dataDataNumbers from the test. in a table so you can compare clearly.

A good data table has the OBJECT in column 1, the DISTANCE in column 2, the TIME in column 3, and (if you want) a NOTES column. Then comparing speeds is just looking at the numbers side by side.

Common misconception: Some people think the object that goes the longest distance must be the fastest. Not always — you have to check the TIME too. A turtle that walks 10 feet in an hour went farther than a sprinter who runs 5 feet in a second, but the sprinter was MUCH faster.

Amazing Florida Fact
A peregrine falcon can dive at over 240 miles per hour — faster than almost any car on the highway. They reach those speeds by tucking in their wings and falling toward their prey at an angle.
🌴

Florida connection: Florida's Daytona International Speedway hosts car races where cars travel about 200 mph. Race officials measure distance and time precisely to determine winners and track every car's speed.

🔭 Go Deeper · What's the difference between speed and velocity?
Speed tells you how fast something moves. Velocity tells you how fast AND in what direction. A car at 30 mph north has a different velocity than a car at 30 mph south, but they have the same speed. In higher grades you'll work with velocity more, but for now, speed is what matters.

🔬 Investigation: Toy Car Speed Test
Distance + time = evidence for who's faster
⏱ 8 min

Race two or three toy objects (cars, balls, marbles) down the same ramp. Measure distance and time. Use your data to decide which one was fastest.

1
Step 1
Set up your ramp

Lean a book or a piece of cardboard against a stack of books to make a small ramp. Use a tape measure or ruler to measure the distance from the bottom of the ramp to a finish line a few feet away.

2
Step 2
Make a data table

Set up 4 columns: OBJECT, DISTANCE, TIME, NOTES. Plan to release 2 or 3 different objects (a toy car, a ball, a marble) one at a time from the SAME spot on the ramp.

3
Step 3
Time the runs

Use a stopwatch (phone is fine). Start when the object leaves the ramp, stop when it reaches the finish line. Record the time. Repeat for each object. (You might want to do each one twice and use the second time.)

4
Step 4
Make your claim

Write one sentence: "I claim that ___ was the fastest." Then write the evidence: "My data shows ___ traveled ___ feet in ___ seconds — faster than ___." That's a complete claim-with-evidence statement.

🤯
Amazing Florida Fact
A NASCAR car at Daytona can travel about 200 mph on the long straightaway — that's about 290 feet per second. Race officials track every car's distance and time with sensors and computers, hundreds of times per second.
🧪
Think like a scientist
"You and a friend race two toy cars down the same ramp. The red car goes 10 feet. The blue car goes 12 feet. You don't know how long it took for either one. Can you tell which was faster yet? Why or why not?"
🔭 Go Deeper · Average vs. instant speed
When you say a car drove 60 miles in 1 hour, that's the AVERAGE speed — it might have gone faster on parts of the trip and slower on others (like at red lights). The speed shown on the speedometer right now is the INSTANT speed. Both are useful, just for different things.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

What TWO things do you need to figure out an object's speed?

AThe object's color and direction
BThe object's weight and shape
CHow far it went (distance) and how long it took (time)
⚡ Right! Speed is always a comparison of distance to time. You need BOTH numbers.
Speed is about how fast — not what color, weight, or shape. To know speed, you need DISTANCE (how far) and TIME (how long).
Q2

Maya runs 20 feet in 5 seconds. Jordan runs 20 feet in 8 seconds. Who is faster?

AMaya — same distance, less time
BJordan — more time means more running
CIt's impossible to tell
🏃 Right! Same distance means whoever finished FASTER (less time) was faster. Maya wins.
More time doesn't mean faster — it means SLOWER (longer to cover the same distance). Maya took less time, so Maya was faster.
Q3

Why should a speed comparison be a FAIR test?

ASo the slowest object always wins
BSo the only thing that's different is the objects you're comparing
CSo everyone agrees who wins, even without measuring
🎯 Right! A fair test keeps the start line, finish line, and surface the same — so only the OBJECTS are different.
A fair test isn't about who wins — it's about making sure the comparison is honest. Everything stays the same EXCEPT the objects you're testing.
