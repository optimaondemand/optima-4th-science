📘 The Lesson: Vibrations Make Sound
Every sound starts with something shaking
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Sound Starts with a Vibration

Pluck a rubber band. Strum a guitar string. Each sound happens because something is moving back and forth very fast. That is a vibrationVibrationFast back-and-forth motion..

The vibration shakes the air around it. Those tiny pushes on the air spread outward as a waveWaveA pattern of moving energy.. When the wave reaches your ear, it shakes your eardrum, and your brain hears it as soundSoundWave energy from a vibration..

Watch out: sound is NOT a tiny thing flying through the air. The air just shakes in place and passes the push along. Be the vibration and watch.

Part 2 · Pitch and Loudness

Some sounds are HIGH (a whistle, a small bird). Some are LOW (a tuba, distant thunder). That's called pitchPitchHigh or low.. Pitch depends on how FAST the source is vibrating: its frequencyFrequencyVibrations per second..

  • Fast vibration → high frequency → HIGH pitch (squeak, whistle).
  • Slow vibration → low frequency → LOW pitch (rumble, big drum).
  • Big vibration → big amplitudeAmplitudeHow big the wiggle is. → LOUD sound.
  • Small vibration → small amplitude → QUIET sound.

A short, tight rubber band makes a HIGH twang. A long, loose one makes a LOW twang. Shorter and tighter means faster vibrations and a higher pitch. Try to tune one by ear.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

What MUST be happening for any sound to exist?

AA light source is shining
BSomething is vibrating
CSomething is hot
📳 Right! Every sound — no exceptions — starts with something vibrating. A guitar string, your throat, a clap, thunder — all vibrations.
Sounds don't need light or heat. They need a VIBRATION. A vibrating source is the one thing every sound has in common.
Q2

Two rubber bands are stretched. One is short and tight; one is long and loose. Which makes a HIGHER pitch when plucked?

AThe long, loose one — bigger means higher
BBoth make the same pitch
CThe short, tight one — it vibrates faster
🎵 Right! A short, tight rubber band vibrates FAST, so it has high frequency and HIGH pitch.
High pitch comes from FAST vibrations. A short, tight rubber band vibrates much faster than a long, loose one — so it makes a higher pitch.
Q3

What makes a sound LOUDER?

AA bigger vibration — bigger amplitude
BA faster vibration — higher frequency
CA colder room
📈 Right! Loudness depends on AMPLITUDE — how big the vibration is. Bigger vibration = louder sound.
Frequency changes the PITCH (high vs. low), not the loudness. Loudness is about how BIG the vibration is — amplitude.
