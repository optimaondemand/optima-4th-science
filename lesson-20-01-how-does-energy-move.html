📘 The Lesson: Four Ways Energy Moves
Heat, light, sound and electrical, each with a source and a receiver
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Energy Travels Between Things

Energy doesn't just sit there. It MOVES. When you put your hand near a campfire, energy moves from the fire to your skin. When sunlight hits a leaf, energy moves from the Sun to the plant. When you yell to a friend, energy moves from your voice to their ear.

Scientists call this transferTransferWhen energy moves from one place to another.. In every transfer, there's a sourceSourceWhere energy comes from. (where the energy comes from) and a receiver (where the energy goes). Spotting both is the start of understanding any energy transfer.

Part 2 · Four Ways Energy Moves

Energy doesn't all transfer the same way. There are four big ways you'll see every day, even in a Florida classroom:

  • HeatHeatEnergy from warmer to cooler.: moves from a warmer place to a cooler place. A hot mug warms your cold hand. A campfire warms your face.
  • LightLightEnergy from a source like the Sun or a lamp.: travels from a source in straight lines until something stops or reflects it. Sunlight reaches a leaf. A flashlight reaches a wall.
  • SoundSoundVibrations through air, water, or solids.: travels as vibrations through air, water, or solids. A drum hits your ear. A whale call travels through ocean water.
  • ElectricalElectrical energyTiny moving charges through a wire. energy: moves through wires as tiny charges flowing. A battery powers a flashlight. A wall outlet powers a lamp.

You can't see most of these energies directly. But you can see what they DO. A warmed cup, a lit-up room, a shaking eardrum, a glowing bulb: each one is evidence that energy traveled from a source to a receiver.

Watch out: Some people think these are four different KINDS of energy, like four flavors. They are really four different WAYS energy can travel.

⚡ Paths for Electricity

Electrical energy needs a path of metal, like a wire. That path is called a circuit. If the path is broken, no energy flows.

Materials that let electricity flow easily, like copper, aluminum and steel, are called conductors. That’s why wires are made of metal! Materials that block it, like rubber, plastic and glass, are called insulators. The plastic coating on a power cord keeps the electricity safely inside the wire.

Air usually blocks electricity, too. Lightning is the giant exception: its push is so strong that it forces a path right through the air.

Quick Self-Check

Q1

What does it mean to TRANSFER energy?

ATo make new energy
BTo destroy energy
CTo move energy from one place to another
➡️ Right! Transfer means energy moving from a source to a receiver.
Energy isn't made or destroyed — it MOVES. Transferring energy means moving it from a source to a receiver.
Q2

A friend shouts your name across the gym. What form of energy moved from their mouth to your ear?

ASound
BHeat
CElectrical
🔊 Right! Sound energy travels through air as vibrations from a source (your friend's voice) to a receiver (your ear).
A shout transfers SOUND energy — vibrations move through the air from your friend's mouth (source) to your ear (receiver).
Q3

In a flashlight, what's the SOURCE of energy?

AThe lightbulb
BThe battery
CThe button
🔋 Right! The battery is where the energy comes FROM. The bulb is the receiver (where the energy ends up as light).
The bulb is the receiver (where the energy ends up). The button is just a switch. The SOURCE — where the energy comes from — is the battery.
