Goals for This Assessment

By the end of this assessment, you'll have shown that you can:

  • Interpret a map to find landforms and resource locations.
  • Use property data (hardness, luster, streak) to identify a rock or mineral.
  • Tell the difference between renewable and nonrenewable resources.
  • Write a clear CER about a resource benefit and its environmental cost.
💡

This isn't a memory test. You can think back to anything you've learned in lessons 4.01 – 8.02. The questions ask you to USE what you know, not just repeat words.

B
⚖️ Part B · Resource Tradeoffs
Every resource use has a benefit AND an environmental cost. Show you can name both.

Every time people use a natural resource, there's something we GAIN and something we LOSE. Below is data about three Florida resources.

ResourceBenefit to PeopleEnvironmental CostRenewable?
Solar energyPowers homes and devices without burning fuelSolar panels take materials to makeYES
Phosphate miningUsed in fertilizer for growing foodDisturbs land and water near minesNO
Limestone quarryingUsed to build roads, buildings, and concreteBig holes in the ground; dust; habitat lossNO
Q5

Pick ONE of the three resources. In your own words, describe the benefit AND the cost. Use evidence from the table.

Q6

Why do scientists say solar energy is RENEWABLE but phosphate is NONRENEWABLE? Use what you've learned about how long each takes to make.

Q7

A community wants to mine more limestone. Suggest ONE protection strategy they could use to reduce the environmental cost. Explain how it helps.

⏳

Weathering and time. Florida's landforms didn't always look the way they do now.

Q8

Florida's beaches are made of tiny grains of sand. Where did all that sand come from? Use the words weathering and erosion in your answer.

🧠CER · Does Using a Resource Always Hurt the Environment?

Write a short paragraph answering: "Does using a natural resource always hurt the environment? Use evidence from this unit." Use what you've learned this unit.

Claim

Write ONE sentence that answers the question.

Evidence

Give TWO pieces of evidence (examples from the lessons).

Reasoning

Explain HOW your evidence supports your claim.
