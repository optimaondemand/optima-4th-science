📘 The Lesson: Two Kinds of Properties
What it IS vs. what happens when it CHANGES
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Physical Properties Describe What It IS

A physical propertyPhysical propertyWhat a material is, without changing it. is something you can observe or measure WITHOUT changing the material into something new. Color, hardness, mass, shape, magnetism and whether it floats are all physical properties.

When you measure the mass of a paperclip or check if a rock is hard, you're still left with a paperclip and a rock at the end. The material itself didn't change. Properties tell you what something IS.

Try it: describe a wooden block without changing it.

Part 2 · Signs of a Possible Chemical Change

A chemical propertyChemical propertyHow a material changes into something new. describes HOW a material reacts and what NEW substance it might make. The clues that a chemical change MIGHT be happening:

  • Color change: a copper roof slowly turning green; bread browning in a toaster.
  • Bubbling or fizz: baking soda dropped into vinegar produces gas.
  • Smoke or light: wood burning gives off smoke, heat, and light.
  • Rust: iron + water + air slowly form reddish rust, a brand-new substance.
  • Heat or temperature change: some reactions release heat; others soak it up.

When you see one of these clues, it's evidenceEvidenceWhat you observe that supports a claim. that a new substanceNew substanceMaterial that wasn't there before. might be forming. The original materialMaterialA kind of matter. isn’t just changing shape. It’s changing INTO something else.

Rotting food and cooking food make new substances too. When a banana rots, tiny living things called mold and bacteria break it down. When pancake batter cooks, it can never go back to runny batter.

Part 3 · Watch Changes Happen

Some changes take seconds. Some take years. Watch four of them, and catch every clue.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Which of these is a PHYSICAL property?

AA piece of bread browning in a toaster
BThe shiny silver color of a steel spoon
CIron slowly rusting outside
🎨 Right! Color is something you can observe without changing what the spoon IS. That's a physical property.
Toasting and rusting are CHEMICAL changes — new substances form. The COLOR of a spoon is just describing what the spoon is like — a physical property.
Q2

Which is the BEST evidence that a chemical change may have happened?

AA piece of paper got smaller after cutting
BAn ice cube melted into water
CA nail turned reddish-brown over weeks left outside
🔍 Right! The reddish-brown color is RUST — a new substance that formed from iron + air + water. Strong evidence of a chemical change.
Cutting paper and melting ice are PHYSICAL changes — the material is still the same after. But rust is a NEW substance that wasn't there before. That's the strongest chemical-change clue.
Q3

A friend says, "All bubbling means a chemical change happened." Is that right?

ANot always — opening a soda makes bubbles but no new substance forms
BYes, every bubble means a chemical change
CNo, bubbles never mean a chemical change
💭 Right! Bubbles are a CLUE — but not proof on their own. Soda fizz is just gas escaping, not a new substance. You need to look at the FULL set of evidence.
Bubbles can be a chemical change clue (like baking soda + vinegar creating gas) — OR they can be physical (carbonated soda just releasing already-dissolved gas). The clue alone isn't enough; check ALL the evidence.
