Case File 14 · A jar from the pond
LR
From Dr. Lena Reyes, field biologist

What will they turn into?

A girl named Ava dropped off this jar at the Field Station. The little wigglers inside hang upside down from the top of the water.

Some insect babies look like small copies of their parents. Others look nothing like them! Before we answer Ava, let’s learn to tell the two insect families apart.

🔄 Changes completely: egg, larva, pupa, adult🪜 Grows up in steps: egg, nymph, adult
I found these wiggly things in our pond. What will they turn into? Ava, age 9

Sort two insect families

Tap the box next to each step when you finish it.

  1. 🖥️Before you put on your headset
  2. Step 1 · Know your families
    Each family has four insects. Type the gray words in the Filter models box. This list is also in VR: open your menu, click Assignments, then click our assignment.
    🔄 Changes completely · in a lineMonarch Butterfly, Ladybug, Firefly, Japanese Beetletype: Monarch · Ladybug · Firefly · BeetleIts baby is a larva that looks nothing like it. Then a pupa.
    🪜 Grows up in steps · in a circleGrasshopper, Cricket, Praying Mantis, Dragonflytype: Grasshopper · Cricket · Mantis · DragonflyIts baby is a nymph: a small copy with no wings. No pupa.
    💡 How to tellAsk: does the baby look like a small adult? No → changes completely.
    Yes, minus wings → grows up in steps.
  3. 🥽In VR
  4. Step 2 · Go in
    Put on your headset and open the On-Demand app. Click the Explore tab and find Tropical Rainforest. (Typing Rainforest in the Filter environments box finds it fast.) Click it, then click Begin Solo. Look around and pick two open spots, a few steps apart.
  5. Step 3 · Line up the “changes completely” family
    Press the top button on your right controller to open your menu, then click the Models tab. Bring out the Monarch Butterfly, Ladybug, Firefly and Japanese Beetle. Set them in a straight line. Insects are tiny, so make each one bigger!
    ✋ To move a model: grab it with the palm trigger (the button under your middle finger). Turn and move it with the thumbstick. Grab it with both palm triggers and pull your hands apart to make it bigger.
  6. Step 4 · Circle up the “grows up in steps” family
    A few steps away, bring out the Grasshopper, Cricket, Praying Mantis and Dragonfly. Set them in a circle and make them bigger too. Then walk to each insect and say why it belongs, like this:
    🗣 “A ladybug changes completely. Its baby is a spiky larva that looks nothing like it.” · “A cricket grows up in steps. Its baby is a nymph that looks like a small cricket with no wings.”
  7. Step 5 · Bonus
    VR onlyAva’s wigglers are baby mosquitoes! Bring out a Mosquito and add it to the family where it belongs. Say out loud how you knew. Skip this step if you run out of time.
  8. Step 6 · Save (capture)
    Stand back so you can see both families. Open your menu and click the Captures tab, then click Capture. Type a title, like your first name. For Class, pick our class (not No class (personal)). For Assignment, pick 14.01 VR · Two Insect Families. Click Save, then look for Submitted at the bottom of the menu.
    🔁 Want to fix something? Move the models and save again. Your new capture takes the place of the old one.
  9. ✅After
  10. Step 7 · Add your caption in Canvas
    Click Leave in the top right corner of your menu and take off your headset. VR can’t hold words, so your caption goes in Canvas. Finish this sentence:
    Then click the green button below the steps when it appears, and paste into the Canvas assignment box. (Or type VR option submitted and your caption yourself.)

Explain your thinking

Write a paragraph (4 or 5 sentences)

Write back to Ava. Tell her what her wigglers are, what they will turn into, and how you know which kind of life cycle they have.

0 of 40 words
Your paragraph needs larva pupa adult complete because
Try these sentence starters:
  • Dear Ava, the wigglers in your jar are ___.
  • Next, each larva will turn into a ___.
  • Then it will come out as an adult ___.
  • I know it changes completely because ___.
  • A cricket is different, because its baby ___.
