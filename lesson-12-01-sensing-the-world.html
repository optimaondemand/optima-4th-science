📘 The Lesson: The Four-Step Pathway
From the world, through the body, to the response
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Stimulus → Sense Organ → Brain → Response

Some body parts have one job: noticing things. They're called sense organsSense organA body part that picks up information from the world.: eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin.

Every time an animal reacts to something in the world, the same four steps happen — in order:

  • 1. StimulusStimulusSomething in the world an animal can notice.. Something happens — a sound, a smell, a flash of light.
  • 2. Sense organ. A body part picks it up.
  • 3. BrainBrainTakes information from the senses, decides what it means, and tells the body what to do.. The brain takes in the informationInformationSomething the animal learns from the world. and decides what it means.
  • 4. ResponseResponseWhat the animal does after its brain decides.. The animal does something.

All four steps can happen in less than one second.

🛑
Stop & Think
"Go back to the SNAP in the forest from the warm-up. Match each step to what happened: stimulus, sense organ, brain, response. Which step did you skip in your first sketch?"

Part 2 · Different Senses for Different Information

Animals don't all notice the same things. A bald eagle spots fish from high in the sky with its sharp eyes. A Florida panther hears the tiny rustle (soft crunching sound) of a deer in the leaves.

  • Eyes pick up light. That’s how you see.
  • Ears pick up sound and vibration (tiny, fast shaking).
  • Nose picks up smell.
  • Tongue picks up taste.
  • Skin picks up touch, heat and cold, and pain.

Watch out for this mistake: The brain can't notice anything by itself. A sense organ has to pick up the information first. No sense organ, no signal. No signal, no response.

⭐ Key Concept

Every animal response is a four-step pathway: stimulus → sense organ → brain → response. Skip a step, and the animal can't react.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Put the four steps of an animal's response in the right order.

ABrain → response → sense organ → stimulus
BSense organ → stimulus → response → brain
CStimulus → sense organ → brain → response
⚡ Right! Something happens (stimulus), a sense organ catches it, the brain decides, and the animal acts.
The pathway always starts with something happening in the world. Then a sense organ catches it, the brain takes the signal, and the animal responds.
Q2

A Florida panther's ear suddenly twists toward a rustling sound. The rustling is the…

AResponse
BStimulus
CSense organ
⚡ Right! The rustling sound is what started the response — that's the stimulus.
The rustling is what the panther noticed — that's the stimulus. The ear is the sense organ. The twist is the response.
Q3

What is the brain's job in the response pathway?

ATake the signal from a sense organ, decide what it means, and tell the body what to do
BBe the stimulus that starts the response
CPick up information from the world
🧠 Right! The brain is the middle step — it takes in the signal, decides what it means, and sends an action message back out.
The brain doesn't start the response — the stimulus does. And the brain doesn't pick up information from the world — that's the sense organ's job. The brain’s job is to decide what the signal means.
