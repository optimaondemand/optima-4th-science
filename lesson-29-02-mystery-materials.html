Collect evidence, compare, identify
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Every Material Has a Property "Fingerprint"

Materials have a unique combination of observableObservable propertyNotice with senses. and measurableMeasurable propertyHas a number. properties, like a fingerprint. Iron is shiny silver-gray, hard, magnetic, and sinks. Aluminum looks similar but is NOT magnetic. Wood is brown, soft, NOT magnetic, and floats. Each materialMaterialUnique kind of matter. has its own pattern.

Scientists keep property guidesProperty guideList of known materials and their properties.: lists of common materials and their properties. When they don't know what an unknown material is, they collect property data and match it to the guide.

Try it: print the fingerprint of Kai’s “SILVER” bolt.

Part 2 · Identification Takes Evidence

To identifyIdentifyMatch unknown to known. an unknown material, you collect evidenceEvidenceWhat you observe. from several tests and compare to the property guide.

  • Observe the appearance: color, shape, finish, smell (only if safe).
  • Measure something: mass with a scale, length with a ruler.
  • Test a behavior: magnetic? floats? bends? scratches glass?
  • Compare to guide: which known material matches MOST of the properties?

The best matches use 3 or more properties that agree. One property can fool you, because lots of things are gray. Three or four properties together build a strong case.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

A mystery material is silver, hard, sinks in water, AND a magnet attracts it. What is it most likely?

APlastic
BAluminum
CIron or steel
🔩 Right! The magnetic test locks it in — only iron, nickel, and steel respond to a magnet. Silver + hard + sinks + magnetic = iron or steel.
Plastic isn't hard and doesn't sink. Aluminum is silver but ISN'T magnetic. The magnet test rules out everything except iron, nickel, and steel.
Q2

Why is it usually NOT enough to identify a material by ONE property?

AProperties always lie
BMany materials share single properties — 'gray' could be many things
CTests are too expensive
🔍 Right! "Gray" could be iron, aluminum, lead, or graphite. You need MULTIPLE properties to tell them apart.
Properties don't lie. Tests aren't expensive. The real reason is that many materials share single properties — you need a combination of evidence to be sure.
Q3

You're testing an unknown material. What's the BEST first step?

AObserve its color, shape, and texture; record what you see
BGuess what it is and write down your guess
CSkip observations and do a fancy test
👀 Right! Real scientists start with careful observations — they don't skip the simple stuff.
Even the simplest observation (color, shape, texture) gives valuable evidence. Skipping that and guessing — or jumping to a fancy test — usually leads to mistakes.
