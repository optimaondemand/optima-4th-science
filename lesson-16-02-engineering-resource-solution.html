🌅 Warm-Up: The Engineer's Job
When there's more than one possible solution
⏱ 5 min

Imagine your Florida city needs more clean drinking water. Engineers and city leaders have to pick a way to get it. Here are three ideas:

  • Idea A: Drill new wells into the aquifer (groundwater).
  • Idea B: Build a plant that takes salt out of seawater.
  • Idea C: Add rules that cut water use citywide by 20%.

"Which idea sounds best to you? But — what would you NEED TO KNOW to choose? What COULD GO WRONG with each one?"

📘 The Lesson: How Engineers Choose
Criteria, constraints, comparison, revision
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Start with Criteria and Constraints

Engineers don’t just look for “the best idea.” First they decide what “best” means, and what they can’t change. Those two things are called criteriaCriteriaWhat a good solution needs to do. and constraintsConstraintA limit on what you can do..

  • Criteria = the goals. A goal can be met well, just OK, or poorly. (Example: “Provide enough water for 10,000 people.”)
  • Constraints = the limits. A limit is pass or fail. (Example: “Must cost less than $50 million.”)

Part 2 · Compare, Choose, Revise

Once you know your criteria and constraints, engineers compare possible solutionsSolutionAn idea that solves part of a problem. side by side. They often use a decision matrix: a chart that scores each designDesignA plan or model for a solution. against each criterion.

  • List each solution across the top.
  • List each criterion down the side.
  • EvaluateEvaluateJudge how well something meets criteria. each cell: Does this solution meet this criterion well? Just OK? Not at all?
  • Compare the totals. Which design scores highest? Which constraints does it break?

The winner usually isn't perfect. That's where revisingReviseChange and improve based on what you've learned. comes in. Engineers look at the gaps and make the chosen solution stronger, maybe by combining ideas or testing a small model first.

Quick Self-Check

Q1

What's the difference between a CRITERION and a CONSTRAINT?

AThere is no difference — they're the same thing
BA criterion is a goal; a constraint is a limit
CA criterion is the cost; a constraint is the benefit
✅ Right! Criteria are what success looks like. Constraints are what you can't do.
Criteria = goals (what success means). Constraints = limits (what can't be changed, like time or budget). Both are important — they're just different.
Q2

An engineer compares 3 solutions, picks one, and then suggests changing it to include parts of another. What is this called?

ADefining the problem
BSetting a constraint
CRevising the design
🔁 Right! Engineers don't just pick — they revise the winner to make it stronger.
That's revision — changing and improving the chosen design. It's a key part of engineering: real solutions almost always get revised.
Q3

Why is it important for engineers to compare at least TWO solutions before choosing?

ASo they can see which one best meets the criteria and constraints
BSo they can spend more money
CSo they can finish faster
🛠️ Right! Comparing solutions is how engineers find the best fit — and often discover ways to revise and combine ideas.
Comparing solutions isn't about money or speed. It's about seeing which solution best meets the goals and limits — and often finding ways to improve the winner.
