📘 The Lesson: Setting Up Energy Stations
Investigate the transfer for yourself
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · What a Station Looks Like

A stationStationA small investigation setup. is a small setup designed to show ONE thing happening clearly. In real research labs, scientists set up stations to test one variable at a time. Today you'll do the same: four stations, one for each way energy moves.

Every station has the same three parts:

  • A sourceSourceWhere energy starts.: something giving off energy (a warm cup, a flashlight, a drum, a battery).
  • A receiverReceiverWhere energy ends up.: something the energy reaches (your hand, a wall, a small object, a bulb).
  • A way to collect evidenceEvidenceWhat supports a claim.: your senses (you can SEE light, HEAR sound, FEEL heat), and sometimes a tool to take a measurementMeasurementA number from a tool..

Part 2 · Strong Evidence vs. Weak Evidence

Anyone can say "I think the cup is warm." Scientists do better than that. They collect EVIDENCE other people can check. Two forms of evidence are especially strong:

  • Direct observation: "I saw the bulb light up." "I heard the drum vibrate." Anyone watching could check.
  • Measurement: "The thermometer read 70°F before, and 85°F after." Now you have a NUMBER to back up your observation. A second person could measure the same way and get a similar number.

The strongest investigation reports use BOTH. You write what you observed, AND you record the measurement. Together they form evidence that another scientist could verify.

Watch out: Some people think real science needs fancy tools. It doesn’t. A thermometer, a ruler, a stopwatch and your own senses are enough for this lesson and for thousands of real investigations.

Part 4 · Run the Stations

Now run all four stations yourself. Do something to each source, then read the tool. Every number you read goes into your station log.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

In an energy-transfer station, what are the THREE parts you always need?

AA battery, a wire, and a bulb
BA source, a receiver, and a way to collect evidence
CA thermometer, a stopwatch, and a ruler
🧪 Right! Every transfer station has a source, a receiver, and evidence collection.
Batteries and bulbs are only used in the ELECTRICAL station. The three parts every station shares are: source, receiver, and a way to collect evidence.
Q2

You tap a drum and watch rice grains bounce on the surface. What's the EVIDENCE that energy transferred?

AThe drum is warm
BThe rice is colorful
CThe rice grains bounced because of the vibrations
🥁 Right! The bouncing rice is evidence that sound energy moved from the drum to the rice.
The color of the rice doesn't tell you about energy transfer. The bouncing — the change you can SEE — is the evidence that vibrations (sound energy) moved from your tap to the drum surface to the rice.
Q3

Why is MEASUREMENT often stronger evidence than just observation?

AA measurement gives you a NUMBER another scientist can check
BMeasurements are always exactly right
CMeasurements use fancier words
📏 Right! Numbers can be checked, repeated, and compared by other people. That's what makes them strong evidence.
Measurements aren't always exact — but they DO give you a number that someone else can repeat or check. Numbers + observation together make the strongest evidence.
