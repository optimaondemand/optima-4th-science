📘 The Lesson: Heat Flows From Hot to Cold
And some materials slow it down
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Heat Flows From Hot to Cold

Touch a metal handrail outside on a sunny day. It feels hot. Now imagine touching it at night when the air has cooled. It feels cold. The handrail itself didn't change — but it gave its heatHeatEnergy moving from warm to cool. energy to the cooler air at night.

Heat is energy that moves between things at different temperaturesTemperatureHow hot or cold.. It always travels in ONE direction — from the hotter object to the cooler one. This is called heat transferTransferEnergy moves between objects..

🛑
Stop & Think
"You're holding an ice cube. Your hand feels cold. Is the ICE making your hand cold, or is your hand giving HEAT to the ice? What's the difference?"

Part 2 · Some Materials Carry Heat — Some Block It

Different materialsMaterialWhat something is made of. handle heat in totally different ways. Metals carry heat extremely well — they're heat conductorsConductorLets heat pass through.. Wood, plastic, foam, and fabric block heat — they're heat insulatorsInsulatorSlows heat down..

  • Good conductors: metals like copper, aluminum, steel.
  • Good insulators: wood, plastic, rubber, foam, dry air, oven mitts.
  • Why it matters: we put metal where we WANT heat to flow (pot bottoms). We put insulators where we DON'T want heat to flow (handles, oven mitts, jackets, walls).

An oven mitt is just an insulator wrapped around your hand. The heat from the pan is real — but the mitt slows it down enough that you have time to set the pan down before your hand gets too hot.

Common misconception: Some people think "cold" is a kind of energy. It's not. Cold is just the ABSENCE of heat. When something feels cold, what's really happening is that YOUR heat is flowing INTO it. Always the same direction — warm to cool.

Amazing Florida Fact
Trapped AIR is one of the BEST insulators on Earth. That's why double-pane windows, puffy jackets, and bird feathers all work — they're mostly just air pockets that keep heat from escaping.
🌴

Florida connection: Florida houses have to insulate against HOT outdoor air, not cold. Builders use foam insulation and reflective barriers to keep heat from getting INTO the house — the opposite job from northern homes.

🔭 Go Deeper · Why do metals conduct heat so well?
Inside metals, particles are packed tightly with lots of free-moving "extra" particles between them. When one end of a metal gets hot, those particles bump into each other very quickly, passing the heat along. That's why a metal spoon can get hot all the way up the handle in just a few seconds.

🔬 Investigation: Cup Race
Which cup keeps water warm longest?
⏱ 8 min

Test which kind of cup is the best heat insulator. Pour warm water into 3 different cups, check the temperature after 10 minutes, and record what you observe.

1
Step 1
Gather 3 cups

Find a METAL cup, a CERAMIC cup, and a FOAM/PLASTIC cup. (If you don't have a metal cup, use a tin can.) Predict which will keep water warm longest.

2
Step 2
Same warm water in each

With an adult's help, pour the SAME amount of warm (NOT hot) tap water into each cup. Note the starting temperature — or just how warm it feels.

3

Wait and observe

Amazing Florida Fact
Penguins have layers of feathers that trap so much air, they can stay warm in -40°F Antarctic weather. The feathers are basically a built-in puffy jacket made of trapped air pockets.
🧪
Think like a scientist
"Why do you wear a coat in cold weather? The coat doesn't MAKE you warm — so what is it actually doing?"
🔭 Go Deeper · Why do animals fluff up when they're cold?
Birds fluff up their feathers, mammals raise their fur (we get "goosebumps" trying), and even cats puff out when chilly. They're all doing the same thing — trapping MORE air between the strands of fur or feathers. More trapped air = better insulator = warmer body.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Which way does heat always move?

AFrom the warmer object to the cooler object
BFrom the cooler object to the warmer object
CIt moves in both directions at the same time
🔥 Right! Heat always flows in ONE direction: from warmer to cooler. That's a rule with no exceptions.
Heat ALWAYS flows from warmer to cooler. "Cold" is just a name for less heat — not a separate kind of energy that flows.
Q2

Which of these is the BEST heat conductor?

AA wooden spoon
BA copper wire
CA foam cup
⚡ Right! Copper is one of the best heat conductors. That's why it shows up in pots, pans, and wires that need to move energy fast.
Wood and foam are INSULATORS — they block heat. Metals like copper let heat flow through easily, so they're conductors.
Q3

A frying pan has a metal bottom and a plastic handle. WHY is it designed that way?

AThe handle is metal because it cooks the food
BThe plastic blocks all heat from the stove
CMetal conducts heat into food; plastic insulates the handle from heat
🍳 Right! The design uses both kinds of materials — a conductor where heat needs to flow, an insulator where heat should NOT flow.
The handle isn't metal — if it were, it would burn your hand. The pan uses METAL on the bottom (conductor) and PLASTIC on the handle (insulator).
