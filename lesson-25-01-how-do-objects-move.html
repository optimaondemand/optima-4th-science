📘 The Lesson: Describing Motion
Position, direction and a reference point
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Position and Reference Point

PositionPositionWhere an object is. is where something IS, compared to something else. You might say "the toy car is 3 feet from the door." The thing you compare to is the reference pointReference pointThe thing you compare to..

If an object's position is the SAME as it was a minute ago, it is at restAt restNot changing position. compared to that reference point. If its position has changed, it's in motionMotionChanging position over time..

In Florida: sailors and surfers pick things on land that stay put, like a lighthouse or a pier, as their reference points. Try it on the beach: find each lost thing from its position clue.

Part 2 · Direction and Path

To describe motion, say how FAR something moved and in what directionDirectionWhich way.. "The toy car moved 5 feet" isn't enough. You have to add which way: "to the right" or "toward the wall."

  • Straight pathPathRoute the object takes.: a ball rolling straight across a floor.
  • Curved path: a Frisbee turning in the wind.
  • Circular path: a Ferris wheel car, or Earth going around the Sun.

A clear description names ALL of it: where it started, which way it went, its path and where it ended. Sam’s delivery robot, Scoot, will test your descriptions. Scoot does exactly what your words say.

Watch out: some people think an object is either moving or not, and that’s that. But motion is a COMPARISON. On the train, you were at rest compared to your seat and in motion compared to the station sign, both at once.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

What do you need to describe an object's position?

AIts color
BIts weight
CA reference point you compare it to
📍 Right! Position is always a comparison to a reference point — "3 feet from the door," "next to the tree," etc.
Position needs a comparison. You can't say where something is without naming what you're comparing it to — that comparison is the reference point.
Q2

You're sitting still on a moving school bus. Compared to your SEAT, are you in motion or at rest?

AIn motion
BAt rest
CBoth at once with no answer
⏸️ Right! Compared to your seat, your position isn't changing — so you're at rest. Compared to the road outside, you're moving fast.
Motion depends on the reference point. Compared to your SEAT, your position stays the same — that's "at rest." (Compared to the road, you'd be in motion.)
Q3

A toy car rolls in a CURVED path around a chair leg, then back to where it started. Which best describes its motion?

AIt changed position but ended where it started — its motion was on a curved path
BIt didn't move at all because it ended in the same spot
CIt moved in a straight path
📐 Right! Motion is about position changing over time. The car DID move — its path was curved, even though it ended where it began.
Even if the car ends where it started, it MOVED through different positions along the way. That counts as motion — and its path was curved.
