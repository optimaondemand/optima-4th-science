📘 The Lesson: Earth's Resources
Discover what makes resources renewable — and what doesn't!
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 — What Is a Natural Resource?

From the moment humans first walked the Earth, we have been searching for and using things found in nature. Everything around you — the wood in your desk, the electricity in the lights, the water in your bottle — started as a natural resourceNatural ResourceAnything found in nature that people use — like water, wood, sunlight, coal, or air. pulled from our planet.

But here's the key question scientists ask: Can we replace it? That one question splits all of Earth's resources into two groups:

♻️
Renewable Resources
Can be replaced or replenished by nature over time. Examples: sunlight, wind, water, forests.
⛽
Nonrenewable Resources
Take so long to form that we cannot replace them in any useful amount of time. Examples: coal, oil, natural gas.
🤔
Stop & Think
"Imagine you have a jar of cookies. If you eat one cookie per day and someone bakes new cookies every morning to replace them, are the cookies renewable or nonrenewable? What if the cookies took 300 million years to bake?"

💡 Use evidence! What does the renewal-time comparison show about why fossil fuels are considered nonrenewable while solar energy is renewable?

👆

Tap on any element in the Resource World — the sun, wind, coal, oil, or more — to learn what makes it renewable or nonrenewable.

🔑 Key Concept

The difference between renewable and nonrenewable is all about TIME. Renewable resourcesRenewable ResourceA resource that can be replaced or replenished naturally over time. come back on their own within a human lifetime — or much faster! Nonrenewable resourcesNonrenewable ResourceA resource that cannot be replaced quickly and can run out. took millions of years to form, so once we use them up, they're gone.

Here's an important warning: even renewable resources can be damaged or used up if we're not careful. If we cut down trees faster than they can grow back, forests can disappear. If we pollute rivers, we can lose our clean water supply.

🤔
Stop & Reason
"If trees are renewable, why do scientists worry about deforestation (cutting down too many trees)? What does that tell you about the phrase 'renewable — as long as we take care of them'?"

Part 2 — Three Important Energy Sources

Energy is one of the most important things we get from natural resources. Let's look at three major energy sources and what makes each one special:

Solar Energy ☀️
♻️ Renewable
Sunlight captured by solar panels. Free, clean, and unlimited!
Wind Energy 💨
♻️ Renewable
Wind turbines turn moving air into electricity. No fuel needed!
Fossil Fuels ⛽
⛔ Nonrenewable
Coal, oil & natural gas formed over millions of years. Once used, they're gone.
🤔
Observe & Reason
"Wind and sunlight are both free and clean. Fossil fuels cause pollution and will run out. So why do you think humans still use so much coal and oil? What might make it hard to switch to renewable energy?"
🏠

Real-World Connection: You may have seen solar panels on rooftops in your neighborhood, or wind farms — huge fields of turbines — along highways. More and more cities are switching from fossil fuelsFossil FuelAn energy-rich material formed from ancient plants and animals over millions of years. to renewable energy sources like solar and wind to help protect our environment.

The key difference between renewable and nonrenewable resources is TIME. Fossil fuels take so long to form that we'll never be able to replace what we use up.
Curious? Go Deeper — What Are Fossil Fuels Actually Made Of?

The word "fossil" might make you think of dinosaur bones — but fossil fuels actually come from something much smaller! About 300 million years ago, Earth was covered in swampy forests and warm, shallow seas filled with tiny organisms.

When those plants and sea creatures died, they sank into mud and were buried under layers of rock. Over millions of years, intense heat and pressure underground transformed those ancient living things into coal, oil, and natural gas.

In other words, when you ride in a gasoline-powered car, you're actually using energy that plants and ocean creatures collected from the sun — hundreds of millions of years ago! Scientists sometimes call fossil fuels "ancient solar energy in storage."

This is also why burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide (CO₂) gas into the atmosphere. That ancient carbon was locked underground for millions of years — and now we're releasing it all at once by burning it.

🔬 Guided Exploration: From Resource to Your Home
Follow the energy from its source all the way to you!
⏱ 8 min

Have you ever flipped on a light switch and wondered where that energy actually comes from? Let's trace the journey of energy from its source all the way to your home — and compare how the path is different for renewable vs. nonrenewable energy.

1

It All Starts with the Sun: Here's something amazing — all of Earth's energy originally came from the sun! Solar energySolar EnergyEnergy that comes from sunlight. Captured by solar panels to make electricity. comes directly from today's sunlight. But even fossil fuelsFossil FuelAn energy-rich material formed from ancient plants and animals over millions of years. are ancient sunlight — stored in plants millions of years ago!

2

Renewable Energy Is Collected: Renewable resourcesRenewable ResourceA resource that can be replaced or replenished naturally over time. are gathered using modern technology — solar panels soak up sunshine, wind turbines spin in the breeze, and hydropower dams capture the energy in flowing rivers. The resource replenishes itself constantly, so we don't have to worry about running out.

3

Nonrenewable Resources Are Extracted: Coal is mined out of the Earth, and oil is drilled from deep underground. These processes can be dangerous and expensive — and they damage the land. Once extracted, fossil fuels are burned to release their stored energy, which releases gases like CO₂ into the atmosphere.

4

Energy Reaches Your Home: Whether it came from a wind turbine or a power plant burning coal, electricity travels through power lines to your home. The lights, the refrigerator, your tablet — all powered by natural resourcesNatural ResourceAnything found in nature that people use — like water, wood, sunlight, coal, or air.. Choosing where that energy comes from matters!

🤔
Think Like a Scientist
"Solar panels and coal plants both make electricity. If renewable energy is cleaner and doesn't run out, why hasn't the whole world switched over already? What challenges might make that difficult?"
💡

Did you know? You can practice conservationConservationThe careful use and protection of natural resources to prevent waste. right now! Turning off lights when you leave a room, taking shorter showers, and walking instead of driving are all ways you help conserve Earth's resources every day.

🤯
Mind-Blowing Fact
A single drop of oil took about 300 million years to form from ancient ocean creatures. At the rate humans use oil today, we burn through what took 1 million years to form — every single month! No wonder scientists say it's nonrenewable.
Curious? Go Deeper — The Problem with Plastic

Oil isn't just burned for energy — it's also the main ingredient in plastic. Every plastic bottle, bag, toy, and straw starts as oil pulled from underground.

Because oil is a nonrenewable resource, using it to make single-use plastic (like straws or plastic bags you throw away after one use) is a double problem: we're using up a limited resource AND creating waste that doesn't break down for hundreds of years.

This is why scientists and engineers are working hard to develop bioplastics — plastics made from plants like corn instead of oil. Plants grow back, so bioplastics could be a more renewable option in the future!
