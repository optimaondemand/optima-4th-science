📘 Review: How Nature Changes Florida
Effects, sinkholes, and tradeoffs
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · One storm, three layers

A hurricane is a natural event. When it causes harm, it is a hazard.

Scientists trace a hazard’s effects in three layers: the land, the living things (the ecosystem), and the people. One effect often ripples into the next. Fallen trees (land) take away bird nests (living things) and block roads (people).

🛑
Stop & Think
"Think of one way a storm changes the land. What living thing does that change next?"

Part 2 · What’s under Florida?

Under most of Florida is limestone, a rock made long ago from shells and sea animals. Rainwater is a tiny bit acidic (a little like very weak lemon juice). As it soaks down, it slowly dissolves the limestone.

Over many years, this leaves hollows (empty spaces) in the rock. Sand and clay sit on top like a lid. If the lid gets too heavy or too wet, or the water underground drains away, the lid falls in. That’s a sinkhole.

In 1981, a giant sinkhole opened in Winter Park, Florida, on a dry spring evening. There was no storm at all. Look closely at what it swallowed.

Part 3 · Every choice is a tradeoff

In January 2010, a hard freeze hit the strawberry farms near Plant City and Dover. To save their plants, farmers sprayed water on them all night. A thin coat of ice kept the berries from freezing solid.

They pumped so much groundwater (water stored underground, in the limestone) that the water level underground dropped about 60 feet. About 140 sinkholes opened, and more than 750 neighbors’ wells went dry for a while. The farmers saved their crop, but the land and their neighbors paid a cost. That’s a tradeoff.

Every choice has two sides. Sort each fact below: is it something people gain, or something given up?

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Heavy rain makes a river flood. What is the heavy rain?

AThe effect
BThe cause
CA constraint
🌧️ Right! The rain makes the flood happen, so it is the cause. The flood is the effect.
A cause makes something happen. Did the rain make the flood happen, or did the flood make the rain?
Q2

How do most Florida sinkholes form?

AEarthquakes crack the ground open.
BWater slowly dissolves limestone and leaves a hollow. Then the ground above falls in.
CHurricane winds blow the sand away.
🕳️ Right! The hollow forms slowly, over years. The ground on top falls in later.
Think about the rock under Florida and what water does to it over many years.
Q3

A town builds a sea wall. It keeps waves away from homes, but the beach in front washes away and sea turtles lose their nesting spot. This is…

Aa tradeoff
Ba criterion
Ca natural event
⚖️ Right! The town gains safer homes and gives up its beach. That’s a tradeoff.
The town gained something and gave something up. What do we call that?
