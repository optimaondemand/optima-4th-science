📘 The Lesson: Building Pathways You Can See
Two animals, two models, one big idea
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · What Makes a Strong Pathway Model

You've already met the four steps. A good modelModelA drawing, build, or explanation of how something works. shows them in order, with arrows between the steps. The strongest models have:

  • An arrow from the stimulus into the sense organ: what the animal noticed.
  • An arrow from the sense organ to the brain, labeled "signal": the message traveling inside the body.
  • An arrow from the brain to the body: what the brain decided.
  • A short caption tying the response to a survival need (food, safety, shelter, mates).
🛑
Stop & Think
"If a model only labels the four parts but has no arrows, is it still a pathway model? Why or why not?"

Part 2 · Two Animals, Two Pathways

The four steps stay the same, but the details change a lot. Here are two Florida hunters that look totally different but follow the same pathway:

  • Bald eagle hunting: sees flash of fish (stimulus) → eye picks it up (sense organ) → brain decides "food, dive!" (processProcessWhat the brain does with a signal.) → eagle dives (response).
  • Florida bat hunting at night: sends out a high-pitched click → click bounces off a moth (stimulus) → bat's ears pick up the echo (sense organ) → brain works out where the moth is (process) → bat swoops and grabs it (response).

Same four-step pathwayPathwayThe steps information follows from world to response.. Totally different ways of doing it. Comparing models shows what is the same for every animal and what is special to each one.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

What does a pathway model need that a labeled drawing alone does NOT?

ABright colors
BArrows showing how the signal moves between steps
CA long paragraph of text
➡️ Right! Arrows show the order and direction of the pathway — that's what makes it a model.
A labeled drawing names parts. A model explains the order of the pathway — and arrows are how it does that.
Q2

If you compare a bat's pathway model and a Florida black bear's pathway model, what stays the SAME?

AThe sense organ they use
BThe exact stimulus
CThe four steps of the pathway
🧩 Right! The steps are universal — stimulus, sense organ, process, response. The details change.
The sense organ and the stimulus are very different between animals. What stays the same is the four-step pathway itself.
Q3

A friend's model shows: stimulus → response, with no other steps. What is missing?

AThe sense organ AND the brain processing the signal
BNothing — the model is complete
CThe arrows
🧠 Right! A real pathway always includes a sense organ picking up the stimulus and the brain processing the signal in the middle.
An animal can't respond if there's nothing to pick up the stimulus and decide what it means. The sense organ and the brain processing the signal are both missing.
