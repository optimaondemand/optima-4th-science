📘 The Lesson: More Than Just Eating
A real ecosystem has many kinds of connections
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Food Chains Are Just the Start

Up to now, every arrow you've drawn between living things has meant the same thing: one ate the other. But if you walked into a Florida forest with that one rule, you'd miss almost everything that's happening.

Look at a single live oak tree. A squirrel eats its acorns — that's a food connection. In spring, the wind carries its pollen from flower to flower — that's pollinationPollinationPollen moves from flower to flower so seeds can form., not eating. A barred owl nests in a hollow (a hole) in its trunk — that's shelter. Mushrooms growing at its base break down fallen leaves into nutrientsNutrientsThe materials plants need to grow. for the soil — that's recycling. (Oaks don't need bees. Bees pollinate other plants, like the saw palmetto that grows under the oak.)

🛑
Stop & Think
"List three things in a Florida forest that touch an oak tree but don't eat it. What is each one doing instead?"

Part 2 · Four Kinds of Connections

Scientists who study ecosystems pay attention to many kinds of connections at once. In every Florida ecosystemEcosystemAll the living and nonliving things in one place., you can usually spot these four:

  • Food connections — who eats whom (you already know these).
  • Pollination — pollinatorsPollinatorAn animal that carries pollen from flower to flower. moving pollen so plants can make seeds.
  • Shelter — a habitatHabitatThe home of a living thing. for nesting, hiding, or resting.
  • Nutrient cycling — fungi (like mushrooms) and bacteria (living things too small to see) breaking down dead leaves and putting nutrients back into the soil.

Put all of these together and you get the web of lifeWeb of lifeAll the connections — eating, pollinating, sheltering, breaking down — that tie an ecosystem together.. It's much bigger than a food web. And humans are part of it too — through farming, building, and protecting natural spaces.

⭐ Key Concept

Living things are connected in many ways — not just by eating. An ecosystem is a web of food, pollination, shelter, and nutrient cycles all happening at once.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

A bee visiting a saw palmetto flower is an example of which kind of connection?

AFood
BPollination
CShelter
🐝 Right! The bee carries pollen from flower to flower — that's pollination.
The bee isn't eating the tree or living in it — it's helping move pollen between flowers. That's pollination.
Q2

Mushrooms breaking down a fallen oak branch and returning nutrients to the soil is an example of…

ANutrient cycling
BPollination
CPredator and prey
🍄 Right! Decomposers return nutrients to the soil so new plants can grow. That's nutrient cycling.
Look at what the mushrooms are doing — breaking dead wood down and feeding the soil. That's nutrient cycling, not pollination or predation.
Q3

All the bees on a Florida blueberry farm disappear. Why is the harvest smaller that year?

AThe plants stopped getting sunlight
BThere was no water
CWithout pollinators, the plants can't make as many blueberries
🫐 Right! No pollinators means most flowers never turn into berries.
The plants still have sun, water, and soil. What they're missing is the bees — the pollinators that move pollen between flowers so fruit can form.
