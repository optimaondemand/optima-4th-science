📘 The Lesson: Body Parts Are Tools
Every structure has a job. Let's match them up.
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Structure Meets Function

Every part of a plant or animal is a kind of tool. A structureStructureA body part of a plant or animal. is the part itself, like a leaf, a beak, a paw or a root. The functionFunctionThe job a structure does. is the job that part does. Structure is the thing. Function is what the thing does.

A leaf is a structure. Its function is to catch sunlight and make food for the plant. A hawk's sharp talons (claws) are a structure. Their function is to grab the animals it hunts. Every organismOrganismAny single living thing. is built from dozens of structures, each doing a job that helps it survive.

🛑
Stop & Think
"Pick one body part you used today — your teeth, your hands, your eyes. What is its function? Now imagine if that part were a different shape. Would it still do the same job?"

Part 2 · Inside and Outside

Structures come in two kinds. ExternalExternalOutside the body. structures are the parts you can see from the outside. InternalInternalInside the body. structures are hidden inside.

  • External plant structures: leaves, bark, flowers, thorns, fruit.
  • Internal plant structures: the inside of the stem that carries water; the inside of a seed.
  • External animal structures: beaks, claws, feathers, shells, eyes, ears.
  • Internal animal structures: heart, lungs, stomach, bones.

Both kinds matter. A turtle's shell (external) protects it from predators (animals that hunt it). The turtle's lungs (internal) let it breathe air, then hold that air on short dives underwater. Take away either one, and survivalSurvivalStaying alive. gets a lot harder.

A mix-up to watch for: Animals don't choose their body parts. Over many, many generations, animals with the most useful parts were the most likely to survive and have babies. The babies got those parts too.

🌵 Plant Survival Adaptations

Plant structures aren't random. They're adaptations (body parts that help a living thing survive where it lives). A desert cactus has thick, waxy skin to hold water and sharp spines to keep animals from eating it. A mangrove tree on Florida's coast has tangled roots that arch above the water and mud. They hold the tree up and take in air. A Venus flytrap grows in poor soil, so it traps insects to get the nutrients (the healthy stuff a living thing needs) the soil doesn't have.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

A leaf is a structure. What is its main function?

ATo hold water for the plant
BTo catch sunlight so the plant can make food
CTo anchor the plant in the soil
🌿 Right! Leaves catch sunlight, which the plant uses to make its own food.
That's the job of a different part. Roots hold the plant in the soil. Leaves are mostly about catching sunlight to make food.
Q2

A gopher tortoise's hard shell is an example of which kind of structure?

AExternal
BInternal
CInvasive
🐢 Right! You can see the shell from the outside — that makes it external.
Look at where the shell is — you can see it from the outside of the tortoise. That makes it an external structure.
Q3

A roseate spoonbill's beak is wide and flat. What's the best evidence-based argument for why?

ABecause the bird thought it would look pretty
BThe flat shape helps it sweep through shallow water and feel small prey
CIt is an invasive species in Florida
🦩 Right! The shape matches the job. The flat beak sweeps water and feels prey the bird can't see.
A good evidence-based argument connects the shape of the structure to the job it does. The spoonbill's flat beak is built for sweeping shallow water and feeling food.
