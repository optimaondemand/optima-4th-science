📘 The Lesson: Same Material, Different Form
Cut it, melt it, fold it — it's still the same stuff
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · A Physical Change Doesn't Make a New Material

A physical changePhysical changeChange in shape, size, or state — not material. changes the look or form of matter, but the MATERIAL stays the same.

Cut a piece of paper into 20 strips — you have paper strips. Same paper. Crumple it into a ball — still paper. Soak it in water and tear it — still paper. MeltMeltSolid to liquid. ice into water and freeze it back — still H2O. DissolveDissolveMix into a liquid. sugar in water and let it evaporate — the sugar comes back.

🛑
Stop & Think
"How do you KNOW a melted ice cube is still water and not something else? What evidence convinces you the material didn't change?"

Part 2 · Reversible vs. Irreversible Physical Changes

Some physical changes are reversibleReversibleCan be undone. — you can return the material to its starting form. Others are irreversibleIrreversibleCan't easily undo. — even though the material is the SAME, you can't easily undo the change.

  • Reversible: melting ice (refreeze), folding paper (unfold), dissolving sugar (evaporate water), changing a state of matterState of matterSolid, liquid, gas..
  • Irreversible-ish: cutting paper into shreds, breaking glass, grinding chalk into dust. Same material, very hard to put back together.
  • Still a physical change: in BOTH cases, no new substance forms. Glass shards are still glass.

The big test for any physical change is to ask: "Is it still the SAME material?" If yes, it's physical. If a brand-new substance has formed, you're looking at a chemical change (that's tomorrow's lesson).

Common misconception: Some people think any irreversible change must be chemical. That's not true. You can't easily un-shred paper or un-break glass, but the material is still paper and glass — physical changes, just hard-to-reverse ones.

⭐ Key Concept

A physical change rearranges or reshapes matter, but doesn't make a new material. It can be reversible (melting ice) or irreversible (breaking glass) — the material stays the same in both cases.

⚖️ Conservation of Matter — Nothing Disappears!

Here's one of the biggest ideas in all of science: matter is never created or destroyed — it just changes form. This is called the conservation of matter. When you tear a piece of paper into tiny bits, you still have the same amount of paper — it's just in more pieces. When you melt an ice cube, the water weighs exactly the same as the ice did.

You can test this yourself: weigh a ball of clay, then flatten it into a pancake and weigh it again. Same mass! The shape changed, but the amount of matter didn't. Even when matter seems to disappear — like water evaporating from a puddle — it hasn't vanished. It turned into water vapor in the air. If you could collect all the vapor, it would weigh exactly what the puddle did. In every physical change, what you start with and what you end with have the same total mass.

Amazing Florida Fact
Florida is famous for boiled peanuts — but did you know boiling peanuts is mostly a PHYSICAL change? The peanut gets soft and full of salty water, but it's still a peanut. Most cooking is a mix of physical AND chemical changes happening at once.
🌴

Florida connection: Florida's sandy beaches are made of countless tiny pieces of broken-down shells, rocks, and glass — all from physical changes over thousands of years. Wave action, wind, and pressure broke big pieces into small ones — but those grains are still the same materials.

🔭 Go Deeper · The three states of matter
Water shows all three states perfectly: ice (solid), water (liquid), steam (gas). Same material, different state. Changing between them is always a physical change — you can melt ice or refreeze water and it's still H2O. In 5th grade you'll go DEEPER into particles and what's happening inside.

Amazing Florida Fact
EVERY DAY, a glass of water in Florida is going through physical changes. It evaporates a little in the heat, condenses on cold surfaces, freezes in your freezer, and dissolves whatever you stir into it. Same H2O, many transformations.
🧪
Think like a scientist
"Florida limestone gets slowly worn down into sand over thousands of years. Is this a physical or chemical change? What stays the SAME?"
🔭 Go Deeper · Mixtures and solutions
A MIXTURE is when you combine two materials and they keep their identities — sand and salt mixed together is still sand AND salt, and you can separate them. A SOLUTION is when one dissolves into another — sugar in water looks like one thing, but it's sugar PLUS water. Both are physical changes.

Q1

Which is the BEST description of a physical change?

AA change that creates a new substance
BA change that always involves heat
CA change in shape, size, or state — but NOT in what the material is
🔁 Right! In a physical change, the form changes but the material stays the same. Cut paper is still paper.
Creating a new substance is a CHEMICAL change. Physical changes are about form, size, or state — and the MATERIAL stays the same.
Q2

Which of these is a REVERSIBLE physical change?

AMelting an ice cube
BCutting a piece of paper into shreds
CBreaking glass
↩️ Right! You can refreeze the melted water to get an ice cube back — that's reversible.
Cutting paper and breaking glass are physical changes you can't easily undo. Melting ice is reversible — just freeze it again.
Q3

You shred a piece of cheese into thin strips. Is this a physical or chemical change?

AChemical — because cheese is now in many pieces
BPhysical — the cheese's shape changed but it's still cheese
CBoth — shredding is always chemical
🧀 Right! The cheese's shape and size changed, but the material is still cheese. That's a physical change.
Just because something is now in many pieces doesn't make it chemical. The material — cheese — is still cheese. That's physical.
