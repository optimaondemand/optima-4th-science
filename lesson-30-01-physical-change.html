📘 The Lesson: Same Material, Different Form
Cut it, melt it, fold it — it's still the same stuff
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · A Physical Change Doesn't Make a New Material

A physical changePhysical changeChange in shape, size, or state — not material. changes the look or form of matter, but the MATERIAL stays the same.

Cut a piece of paper into 20 strips and you have paper strips. Same paper. MeltMeltSolid to liquid. ice into water, then freeze it back. Still water. DissolveDissolveMix into a liquid. sugar in water, let the water dry up, and the sugar comes back.

Try it: call the number before the scale does.

Part 2 · Reversible vs. Irreversible Physical Changes

Some physical changes are reversibleReversibleCan be undone.: you can put the material back the way it started. Others are irreversibleIrreversibleCan’t easily undo.. The material is still the SAME, but you can’t easily undo the change.

Melting ice and folding paper can be undone. Shredding paper and breaking glass cannot. Either way, no new material forms.

The big test for any change is one question: is it still the SAME material? If yes, the change is physical. If a brand new material has formed, like wood turning to ash when it burns, iron turning to rust, or an egg cooking, that is a chemical change.

Common misconception: Some people think any irreversible change must be chemical. That's not true. Un-shredded paper and un-broken glass are impossible, but both are still the same material.

Part 3 · Nothing Disappears

⚖️ Conservation of matter

Here is one of the biggest ideas in all of science: matter is never made and never destroyed. It only changes form. Melt an ice cube and the water weighs exactly what the ice did.

Even when matter seems to disappear, it hasn’t. A puddle dries up in the sun, but the water didn’t vanish. It turned into water vapor (water as a gas) in the air, and it still weighs what the puddle did.

People have used that for thousands of years. Look at what the sun leaves behind.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Which is the BEST description of a physical change?

AA change that creates a new substance
BA change that always involves heat
CA change in shape, size, or state — but NOT in what the material is
🔁 Right! In a physical change, the form changes but the material stays the same. Cut paper is still paper.
Creating a new substance is a CHEMICAL change. Physical changes are about form, size, or state — and the MATERIAL stays the same.
Q2

Which of these is a REVERSIBLE physical change?

AMelting an ice cube
BCutting a piece of paper into shreds
CBreaking glass
↩️ Right! You can refreeze the melted water to get an ice cube back — that's reversible.
Cutting paper and breaking glass are physical changes you can't easily undo. Melting ice is reversible — just freeze it again.
Q3

You shred a piece of cheese into thin strips. Is this a physical or chemical change?

AChemical — because cheese is now in many pieces
BPhysical — the cheese's shape changed but it's still cheese
CBoth — shredding is always chemical
🧀 Right! The cheese's shape and size changed, but the material is still cheese. That's a physical change.
Just because something is now in many pieces doesn't make it chemical. The material — cheese — is still cheese. That's physical.
