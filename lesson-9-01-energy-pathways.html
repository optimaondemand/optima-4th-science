📘 The Lesson: Tracing Energy from the Sun
Where does the energy in every living thing actually start?
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · The Sun is the Starting Point

Every animal on Earth eats food. Food gives an animal the energyEnergyThe ability to do work or cause change. it needs to move, grow, and stay warm. But here's the part that surprises a lot of people: animals can't make their own food. Not a hawk, not a shark, not you.

So where does the energy come from? Follow the trail backward and it almost always leads to the same place: the Sun. Sunlight is the giant battery that nearly all life on Earth runs on.

Part 2 · Plants Are the Middle Step

Plants take in sunlight, pull water up through their roots, and take in a gas from the air called carbon dioxide. Then they use the sunlight's energy to put those pieces together and build their own food, a kind of sugar. We call this process photosynthesisPhotosynthesisHow plants use sunlight + water + air to make their own food..

Because plants make their own food, scientists call them producersProducerA living thing — usually a plant — that makes its own food using sunlight.. Producers are the doorway: they catch energy from the Sun and turn it into food that animals can eat.

🌱 What Do Producers Need?

Producers need four things: sunlight (the energy), water (pulled up through their roots), carbon dioxide from the air (taken in through tiny holes in their leaves), and minerals from the soil (nutrients like nitrogen that help them grow). Take away any one, and the plant can’t make enough food. Then every animal that depends on it goes hungry.

When a grasshopper eats a plant, the energy stored in that plant gets passed into the grasshopper. When a green tree frog eats the grasshopper, the energy gets passed again. The grasshopper and the frog are both consumersConsumerA living thing that gets its energy by eating other living things., but the energy started in the Sun and traveled through the plant first. A gopher tortoise munches grasses and wildflowers, so its energy started in the Sun too.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Where does almost all the energy in a Florida food chain begin?

AThe Sun
BThe soil
CThe water
☀️ Yes! Plants capture sunlight first, and the energy flows from there into every animal in the chain.
Not quite. Soil and water help plants grow, but the energy in the food chain comes from sunlight. Plants are the doorway.
Q2

A green sea turtle eats sea grass. Why do scientists call the sea turtle a consumer?

AIt lives in the water
BIt gets its energy by eating another living thing
CIt makes its own food from sunlight
🐢 Right! Any animal that has to eat something else to get energy is a consumer — even ones that only eat plants.
Close — but the key isn't where it lives or whether it makes food. A consumer is any living thing that gets energy by eating another living thing.
Q3

A diagram shows: Sun → sawgrass → grasshopper → frog. The sawgrass is the…

AConsumer
BDecomposer
CProducer
🌿 Exactly! The sawgrass uses sunlight to make its own food. It's the producer — the first living step in the chain.
Not quite. The sawgrass is making its own food using sunlight, which makes it a producer. Consumers eat; decomposers break things down.
