What You'll Show
Goals for This Assessment

By the end of this assessment, you'll have shown that you can:

  • Pick your best model from the year and explain why it works.
  • Pick your best investigation and describe the evidence it gave you.
  • Pick your best CER and revise it with what you know now.
  • Reflect on your growth as a scientist.
  • Set a goal for next year.
💡

This isn't a memory test. You can think back to anything you've learned in lessons all year. The questions ask you to USE what you know, not just repeat words.

How to Submit
Type, Gather, Copy, Paste

Each question has a typing box. When you're done, jump to Part C, click "Gather All My Answers", then copy and paste them into the Canvas submission box. That's it — no recording, no photos, just your words.

A
🔍 Part A · Your Best MODEL from the Year
Pick a model you drew, labeled, or built this year — and explain why it works.

All year you've made models — food webs, the water cycle, the rock cycle, motion diagrams, energy conversions, the flowering plant reproduction cycle, sound wave diagrams, parent/offspring trait charts, hazard cause/effect, and more.

💡

Pick ONE model you remember making well. It could be one you drew on paper, one you built with tape, or one you put together in a notebook.

Q1

Describe your chosen model. What did it show? What were the main parts?

Q2

Why did you pick THIS model? What did it teach you that you remember today?

Q3

If you could improve your model now, what would you change? What's ONE thing you understand BETTER now than when you first made it?

B
🧪 Part B · Your Best INVESTIGATION from the Year
Pick a hands-on test you ran this year — and describe what the evidence taught you.

All year you've run investigations — pinwheels and water wheels for renewable energy, ruler tests for sound and pitch, ramps and toy cars for motion, magnet tests on household items, ice melting and dissolving sugar, predicting collisions, identifying mystery materials, and more.

🔬

Pick ONE investigation you remember running — even a small one at home counts.

Q4

What was the question you were trying to answer? Describe what you did, step by step.

Q5

What EVIDENCE did you collect? What did the data tell you?

Q6

Did anything SURPRISE you during this investigation? Did your prediction match what actually happened? What did you learn from a "wrong" prediction (if you had one)?

C
📝 Part C · CER + Reflect + Submit
Pull it all together with a Claim-Evidence-Reasoning paragraph and a short reflection.

Scientists can't visit the Moon every night. They can't fly to Mars to study its surface. But they STILL learn about these places.

🧠CER · Choose Your Strongest CER From the Year

Write a short paragraph answering: "Pick one of the BIG questions you wrote a CER about this year. (Examples: How does energy get from the Sun to a panther? Why does the moon look different each night? Does using a resource always hurt the environment? What makes a change CHEMICAL?) Rewrite your strongest CER now — with everything you know." Use what you've learned this unit.

Claim

Write ONE sentence that answers the question.

Evidence

Give TWO pieces of evidence (examples from the lessons).

Reasoning

Explain HOW your evidence supports your claim.

🔄

Revise Your Thinking. If you started the unit with a different idea about how scientists work — write it below. Then describe what you think NOW after this unit.

Q9 · Reflection

Write 3–4 sentences answering: How have you GROWN as a scientist this year? What can you do now that you couldn't do in August? What's ONE goal you have for science learning in 5th grade?
