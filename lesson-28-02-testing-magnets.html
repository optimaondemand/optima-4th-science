📘 The Lesson: Running a Magnet Test
Predict, test, record, sort
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Make a Prediction First

Real scientists don't just touch things with a magnet and see what happens. They make a predictionPredictionA reasonable guess before testing. first. Based on what you already know (magnets attract iron, nickel and steel), you can usually guess whether something is magneticMagneticA magnet attracts it. or nonmagneticNonmagneticA magnet ignores it..

Predicting first shows what you ALREADY know. And when a prediction is WRONG, that’s where you learn the most. Try it: predict, say how sure you are, then test.

Part 2 · Use a Data Table

A data tableData tableOrganized record of results. keeps your results clear and easy to compare. For a magnet test, a good table has 4 columns: MATERIAL, PREDICTION, RESULT, and NOTES.

  • Material: name of the object you're testing.
  • Prediction: what you think will happen BEFORE you test.
  • Result: what actually happens when the magnet comes close (sticks / nothing).
  • Notes: anything you noticed (the magnet only pulled the metal PART of an item, etc).

When you're done, sort the materials into two groups based on RESULTS: attractedAttractPull together. (magnetic) and not attracted (nonmagnetic). Then look for patterns: what do all the magnetic items have in common?

Mo didn’t use a table. Mo used sticky notes. Help turn them into a real data table.

📋 A Magnet Test Data Table
A good investigation always ends with a sort. Group the magnetic items together, and look for what they have in common.

Part 3 · Make It a Fair Test

A fair test changes only ONE thing. Everything else stays the same: the same magnet, the same distance, the same way of moving it. Then, if the results are different, you know why: it was the material.

Part 4 · The 8-Material Fair Test

Now run a real fair test. Lock the settings first. Then slide each item toward the magnet along a ruler, and record where it jumps.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Why should scientists make a PREDICTION before they test?

ASo they can compare what they expected to what actually happened
BSo they always look smart
CSo they know what to test next
🎯 Right! Predictions let scientists compare expected vs. actual — that's where the LEARNING happens.
A prediction lets you compare what you THOUGHT would happen to what ACTUALLY happens. The difference is where the learning is — especially when you guess wrong.
Q2

A magnet attracts a steel paperclip but NOT a copper penny. Both are metal. What does this evidence show?

AMagnets only work on small things
BThe penny is broken
CNot all metal is magnetic — it depends on what KIND of metal
🔩 Right! Steel (iron-based) is magnetic; copper isn't. Magnets only attract certain metals — iron, nickel, and steel.
Magnets work on objects of any size that contain iron, nickel, or steel. Copper isn't magnetic. The evidence shows that NOT all metal is magnetic.
Q3

Why is a DATA TABLE important in a magnet test?

AIt makes the magnet stronger
BIt organizes results so you can compare and find patterns
CIt hides the predictions you got wrong
📋 Right! A clean data table lets you SEE patterns — like which materials are magnetic vs. nonmagnetic — at a glance.
A data table organizes information so you can spot patterns and back up your conclusions. It does NOT change anything about the magnet itself or hide your data.
