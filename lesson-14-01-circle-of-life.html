📘 The Lesson: Patterns of Growth
Different cycles, same big idea
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Every Living Thing Has a Life Cycle

You've studied how plants and animals are built and how they sense the world. Today we step back and look at the whole picture: every organism goes through a life cycleLife cycleThe whole path from birth to making the next generation.. It starts with the beginning — an egg, a seed, or a tiny baby — and ends with the same organism making offspringOffspringThe new generation an organism makes. of its own. Then the cycle starts over.

Plant cycles look different from animal cycles, but the big steps are similar: start → grow → make the next generation. A sunflower seed grows into a stem, then a leafy plant, then a flowering plant, then makes new seeds. A monarch butterfly hatches from an egg, eats, changes its body, becomes a butterfly, and lays new eggs.

🛑
Stop & Think
"Pick any Florida plant or animal you know. What's its very first stage? What's its last stage before it makes offspring of its own?"

Part 2 · Two Kinds of Metamorphosis

Insects in Florida go through two main kinds of life cycles. The kind they go through is called metamorphosisMetamorphosisA big change in an animal's body during its life cycle. — a Greek word that means "change of shape."

  • CompleteComplete metamorphosisFour very different stages: egg, larva, pupa, adult. metamorphosis — four stagesStageOne step in a life cycle.: egg → larva → pupa → adult. The young (larva) looks completely different from the adult. Butterflies, bees, and beetles all go through complete metamorphosis.
  • IncompleteIncomplete metamorphosisThree stages: egg, nymph, adult. metamorphosis — three stages: egg → nymph → adult. The young (nymph) looks like a smaller version of the adult and grows bigger as it sheds its skin. Grasshoppers, dragonflies, and cockroaches go through incomplete metamorphosis.

Birds, mammals, and reptiles don't go through metamorphosis at all. Their babies look like smaller versions of the adults from the start — a Florida bear cub looks like a tiny bear, not a different animal.

Common misconception: Some people think "metamorphosis" means any kind of growing up. It really means a big shape change — like a caterpillar turning into a butterfly. A puppy growing into a dog is just growth, not metamorphosis.

Amazing Florida Fact
Inside its chrysalis, a monarch caterpillar literally turns into liquid before rebuilding itself as a butterfly. The body parts you see in the adult are not the same parts the caterpillar had — they're newly grown.
🌴

Florida connection: Florida is right on the monarch butterfly's great migration route. Every fall, monarchs travel thousands of miles south through Florida toward Mexico — and the next spring, several life cycles later, their great-grandchildren make the trip back north. One species, many cycles, one journey.

🔭 Go Deeper · Why do some animals have such fast life cycles?
A Florida mosquito can go from egg to adult in about a week. A Florida bald eagle takes 4–5 years to be ready to make offspring. Faster cycles let mosquitoes make millions of new mosquitoes quickly. Slower cycles give larger animals time to grow strong and learn before they raise young.

Amazing Florida Fact
A Florida cicada can spend up to 17 years underground as a nymph before crawling up, shedding its skin, and emerging as an adult — all to live for just a few weeks above ground.

Think like a scientist
"A Florida scientist finds a strange insect with wings, then six months later finds what looks like a totally different bug in the same spot. How could she use the idea of metamorphosis to investigate whether they might be the same species at different stages?"
🔭 Go Deeper · Why do Florida sea turtles have such long life cycles?
A loggerhead sea turtle hatched on a Florida beach takes about 30 years to grow big enough to lay its own eggs. That's why protecting sea turtle nests is so important — every adult that comes back to lay eggs traveled for decades to get there. If we lose adults, the next generation takes a very long time to replace.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

Put the stages of a monarch butterfly's life cycle in the right order.

ACaterpillar → egg → butterfly → chrysalis
BEgg → caterpillar → chrysalis → adult butterfly
CAdult butterfly → chrysalis → egg → caterpillar
🦋 Right! It starts with the egg, hatches into a caterpillar, becomes a chrysalis, and emerges as an adult.
Every life cycle starts at the beginning of the new generation. For a monarch, that's the egg, then caterpillar, then chrysalis, then adult.
Q2

What's the BIGGEST difference between complete and incomplete metamorphosis?

AComplete metamorphosis has a chrysalis or pupa stage; incomplete does not
BIncomplete metamorphosis has more stages than complete
COnly complete metamorphosis starts with an egg
🐛 Right! The big difference is the pupa/chrysalis stage where the body completely changes shape.
Look again: complete has FOUR stages (with a pupa); incomplete has THREE stages (no pupa). Both start with an egg.
Q3

A Florida bear cub looks like a tiny bear. A monarch caterpillar looks nothing like a butterfly. Why?

AThe bear is invasive and the butterfly is not
BThe bear is younger
CMammals don't go through metamorphosis; butterflies do (complete metamorphosis)
🦋 Right! Mammals grow without changing body shape. Butterflies go through complete metamorphosis, which means the caterpillar and adult look totally different.
The bear and butterfly are both native Florida animals. The real difference is that mammals don't go through metamorphosis — but butterflies do (complete metamorphosis).
