📘 Lab: Test, Score, Improve
Be the town’s engineer
⏱ 15 min

Part 1 · Run the test tank

Engineers can’t wait for a real hurricane to test a plan, so they use a model (a small version of the real thing). Our test tank is a model of Bay Street: the ocean on one side, a plan in the middle, and the homes on the other.

Try each plan in the big storm. Watch how much water reaches the homes.

Part 2 · Score the plans

A plan doesn’t win just because it keeps out the most water. It has to meet the goals AND stay inside the limits. A constraint is a must: a plan that breaks one can’t be picked, even if it scores well.

Use your test results to score the sea wall and the living shoreline.

🛑
Stop & Think
"Why might a town choose a plan that keeps out a little LESS water?"

Part 3 · Make it better

Even the best plan has a weak spot. Engineers revise: they change the design to fix the weak spot, then test it again.

Find the living shoreline’s weak spot. Then pick a fix and re-run the big storm.

Quick Self-Check
Not graded — just for you!
Q1

“The plan must cost $2 million or less.” What is this?

AA criterion (a goal)
BA constraint (a limit)
CAn effect
🚧 Right! A money limit is a constraint. A plan must stay inside it.
Is this something the plan should DO, or a LIMIT the plan has to stay inside?
Q2

Mangroves and oyster reefs that calm waves are what kind of mitigation?

ANatural
BBuilt
CPlanning
🦪 Right! They are living things, so this is natural mitigation.
Mangroves and oysters aren’t made of concrete, and they aren’t a plan on paper. What are they?
Q3

Engineers tested a plan, found a weak spot, and added a sand dune. What did they do?

AThey revised the design.
BThey made a constraint.
CThey caused a hazard.
🔁 Right! Fixing a weak spot and testing again is revising.
They changed the plan to make it better. What is that called?
